# Sidestep

**Play online:** https://xanhub1.github.io/sidestep/

A dependency-free HTML5 football dodge game. An overhead view shows a blue cylinder running through a continuous football field. Cut around pursuing defenders, earn dodge bonuses, and cross the end zone into the next field.

## Play locally

Run `npm.cmd start` on Windows, or `npm start`, then visit **http://localhost:4173**. JavaScript modules require an HTTP server.

Choose Easy, Medium, or Hardcore. The player runs upfield automatically. **Swipe or drag anywhere** to steer in any direction: up and down move freely across the visible pitch. Release to keep the chosen screen position while the field scrolls. Arrow keys and **W / A / S / D** also work; **Space** or **Escape** pauses.

Defenders arrive in small groups from different random directions. Easy starts with 3-4 per group, Medium with 5-6, and Hardcore with 7-8, subject to spacing and the population cap. Each defender starts fully outside the visible screen and runs immediately. Arrows follow offscreen defenders and grow as they approach. There is no stationary warning timer or visible fade-in. They intercept, then accelerate smoothly into pursuit after a miss at about 94% / 99% / 103.5% of the running pace on Easy / Medium / Hardcore. Once passed, defenders have only a short recovery window (0.25 / 0.4 / 0.55 seconds), then slow over roughly one second to 22% / 28% / 34% of the running pace and peel toward the nearest side. At most 1 / 2 / 3 defenders can sustain a trailing pursuit; others disengage sooner. They retire only after their whole body is offscreen. This clears the center lane even when the runner stays near the top of the view. They remain collidable until they leave the camera's surrounding area.

A nearby defender successfully evaded awards **+50 DODGE** once. A touchdown awards **+500** once per field. Each new yard gained earns **+1 RUN**. Previously gained ground cannot award points again; the final score adds run, dodge, and touchdown points. Easy begins with red opponents on grass. Medium begins at the faster white-defense stage on a brown field. Hardcore begins at the faster elite stage, with dark opponents on deeper green turf. Later fields retain the elite team instead of cycling back to red. Each touchdown increases opponent speed, pursuit, interception, arrival frequency, and population limits. Group sizes rise every second touchdown. Earlier pursuers retain their team across a touchdown. One tackle ends the run with a short dust-cloud animation and a score breakdown.

Sound and haptics have separate toggles. Audio unlocks on interaction; unsupported vibration is optional. Per-difficulty best scores are saved locally under a new scoring-version key. Previous best-score data is preserved. Switching away from the browser pauses the run.

## Movement and field model

`dist/engine.mjs` owns world coordinates, controls, following-camera state, pursuit, group arrivals, swept collisions, and scoring. The field is 32 world units wide, about twice the sideways room of the 16.5-unit screen, each playable length is 200 units, and end zones are 14 units long. A runner advances at 8.32 / 10.24 / 12.16 units per second on Easy / Medium / Hardcore. Additional two-axis steering defaults to 14.88 units per second, 20% quicker than version 2.5, with normalized diagonals and smooth settling. Open the gear button or Game settings in the pause menu to adjust steering speed from 60% to 200%, adjust smoothing from 0% to 100%, or turn smoothing off entirely for direct response. Defaults are 120% speed and 50% smoothing. Preferences save locally, survive reloads and retries, and can be reset without affecting scores. Opening settings pauses an active run.

Kickoff places the runner at 90% of the canvas height and the starting white line at 98.5%. The camera scrolls at the running pace. Horizontally it holds still while the runner stays in the middle third of the screen (34% to 66% of the width). Further out, the field eases into the runner's sideways speed and matches it at 22% / 78%, until the sideline comes into view. It moves only while the runner heads outward, so it stops with the runner and never drifts back: a cut changes the screen position without subsequent recentering on either axis. While a drag is held, its target slides with the field so the runner stays under the finger; holding beyond the easing band keeps the runner moving toward that sideline. Vertical targets are bounded at the screen edges. Turf markings, actors, and dodge effects use the same world-to-screen transform with mild perspective. Rendering interpolates both the camera and actors between 120Hz physics updates. Horizontal bounds are the pitch edges; at a sideline the runner sits about 91% of the way across the screen, with the line visible. Vertical bounds keep the player visible between 7.5% and 94% of the canvas height. Pointer deltas use the inverse projection for consistent sensitivity across the screen; resizing preserves both axes of the chosen screen position.

Difficulty sets the starting stage, defender speed, arrival interval, and population limit immediately. Starting approach speeds are 4.96 / 8.48 / 10.8 units per second, and group intervals are 2.625 / 2.0625 / 1.5625 seconds before variation. Medium and Hardcore anticipate cuts more strongly and turn faster. Population limits start at 22 / 36 / 50 and increase after touchdowns. The slower world pace and responsive free steering are preserved. Score and completed touchdowns increase pressure without resetting. Character bodies and collision footprints are about 22% larger. Spawn geometry uses the full projected body bounds. Offscreen entries already move and collide; first visibility is a continuous boundary crossing. Rear entries briefly sprint into view before settling into pursuit. Local separation keeps packs from stacking together. Offscreen defeated pursuers eventually leave the active population, allowing fresh waves to continue. Defender turning is smoothed, interceptors lead the player, and pursuers continue seeking the player in world space. Falling behind follows from relative speed; the engine never forces a pursuing defender to run downfield.

## Validate and host

Run `npm.cmd test` for the engine suite and `npm.cmd run check` for syntax checks. With the local server running, `node tests/browser-smoke.mjs` exercises the playable game through locally installed Chrome: desktop and mobile layouts, touch and mouse steering, automatic running, camera interpolation, scoring, stage transitions, tackle/results, saved best, pause/help, and natural attacks. Screenshots and results go to the ignored `artifacts/` directory. Set `CHROME_PATH` for another Chrome installation.

No packages or build step are required. Deploy `dist/` to a static host with JavaScript module MIME support. Every push to `main` runs the engine tests and syntax checks, then publishes `dist/` to GitHub Pages through `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. The existing private Sites project is declared in `.openai/hosting.json`.
